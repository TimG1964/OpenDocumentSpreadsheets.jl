Coming from XLSX.jl

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets follows XLSX.jl's interface, so most code needs only its file names changed:

XLSX.jlOpenDocumentSpreadsheets
XLSX.readxlsx, XLSX.openxlsxreadods, openods
XLSX.newxlsx, XLSX.writexlsx, XLSX.savexlsxnewods, writeods, saveods
XLSX.streamxlsxstreamods
XLSXFile, XLSXErrorODSFile, ODSError

These work as in XLSX.jl: readtable, gettable, eachtablerow, readdata, getdata, indexing, getcell, getcellrange, eachrow, iserror, geterror, setdata!, writetable, writetable!, addsheet!, writerow!, writerows! and the Tables.jl interface.

The formatting functions work as in XLSX.jl too, with the differences listed in Formatting:

  • setFont, setFill, setBorder, setAlignment, setFormat and their getters
  • the setUniform* variants and setOutsideBorder
  • getText
  • mergeCells and the other merge functions
  • setColumnWidth, setRowHeight, their getters and autofitColumns
  • writetable's column_widths and column_formats

Differences

  • References print in LibreOffice's notation (Sheet1.A1); Excel's (Sheet1!A1) is still accepted. Lists of areas can also use ~.
  • Formulas read and write in LibreOffice's notation (=SUM(Sheet2.A1:B2,1)), not Excel's.
  • Durations: a time cell of 24 hours or more, or a negative one, reads as a Dates.CompoundPeriod, not a Time.
  • Cells: a Cell holds its decoded value, its value type as a symbol, its style's name and its formula text, rather than XLSX.jl's internal indices.
  • Error codes are the text LibreOffice shows, including its own Err:NNN.
  • Saving an edited file keeps everything not edited, rather than rewriting the file.
  • display_text=false (no XLSX.jl counterpart) writes smaller files; see Saving and writing.
  • Formatting works on ODF's styles: setters return a style name, getters work on read-only files, and empty cells and whole columns are formatted as LibreOffice formats them. OpenDocumentSpreadsheets also has named cell styles (setCellStyle, addCellStyle!), which XLSX.jl lacks. See Formatting.
  • Inserting and deleting rows and columns (insertRows, insertColumns, deleteRows, deleteColumns) take XLSX.jl's arguments and behave as LibreOffice does: a deleted reference is stored as #REF! without its sheet, 3-D references follow, new rows copy the formatting of the row above (borders included), and new columns that of the column to their left, with the width of the column they push right.
  • Sheet operations (renamesheet!, deletesheet!, copysheet!, movesheet!) take XLSX.jl's arguments and behave as LibreOffice does; a copy repoints only references whose sheet name is relative, and a name already in use is an error.
  • Defined names (addDefinedName, deleteDefinedName, deleteAllDefinedNames, getDefinedNames, getAllDefinedNames) take XLSX.jl's arguments, with LibreOffice's rules: a name may hold ? but not \, and names such as C1x that LibreOffice reads as references are refused. A sheet's name may refer to another sheet, whole columns and rows can be named, and Bool constants stay Bool. A formula's sheet names without $ are relative, as in LibreOffice. ODF has no system names, so force and include_system change nothing.
  • Hiding (setRowHidden, setColumnHidden, setSheetVisibility) and tab colours (setTabColor) take XLSX.jl's arguments. ODF has no very hidden sheets: :veryHidden hides a sheet as :hidden does, as LibreOffice imports Excel's. Theme colours are not accepted, as elsewhere in OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.
  • Grouping (groupRows, groupColumns, ungroupRows, ungroupColumns) takes XLSX.jl's arguments, but follows LibreOffice, whose groups are nested containers rather than Excel's levels per line: groups next to each other stay separate, ungrouping removes a whole group (and lifts the groups inside it), and a range that crosses a group, or an eighth level, is an error.
  • Panes and the sheet view (freezePanes, splitPanes, setSheetView, getSheetView) take XLSX.jl's arguments and are kept as LibreOffice keeps them, in its view settings: the page-break view, row and column headers and zeros are the whole file's; a selection is kept as its cursor only; zoom runs from 20 to 600; LibreOffice has no page layout view and no "frozen split" (splitFreeze freezes). A split's position is in screen pixels, so it falls at the edge of the rows and columns given on a 96-dpi screen at 100% zoom.
  • Protection (protectSheet, protectWorkbook, setCellProtection and their getters) takes XLSX.jl's arguments, stored as LibreOffice stores it. Passwords are hashed as LibreOffice hashes them (SHA-1 of the UTF-16 text), so any text works, not only 15 ASCII characters. LibreOffice has no permission to format, sort, insert hyperlinks or edit objects and scenarios on a protected sheet, and no window protection: asking for them is an error.
  • Printing (setPageSetup, setPageMargins, setPrintOptions, setPrintArea, setPrintTitles, setHeaderFooter and their getters) takes XLSX.jl's arguments, Excel's header codes included, and stores everything as LibreOffice's xlsx import stores it: a page style of the sheet's own (PageStyle_<sheet>), ODF fields and text styles for the codes, table:print-ranges and header-row containers. paperSizes are those the import knows (Excel's 1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13 and 70), and any other size is paperWidth and paperHeight; headers can't be told not to scale or align with the margins (scaleWithDoc, alignWithMargins), as in LibreOffice. A sheet without a page style of its own prints LibreOffice's default header and footer, and getHeaderFooter says so.
  • Document properties (setDocumentProperties, setCustomProperty and their getters) take XLSX.jl's arguments, stored in meta.xml as LibreOffice's xlsx import stores them. ODF has no category, content status, company or manager: they are the custom properties the import makes of them (OOXMLCorePropertyCategory, OOXMLCorePropertyContentStatus, Company, Manager), which getCustomProperties leaves out. Custom numbers are ODF's floats (a whole one is read back as an Int64), and custom properties are kept in name order, as LibreOffice keeps them. getCustomProperties gives a Vector of pairs, not an OrderedDict.
  • Tables (addtable!, settotals!, appendtable!, table, readtable(…; table_name), writetable(…; as_table) and the rest) are LibreOffice's database ranges. LibreOffice has no table styles (style is an error) and keeps no totals settings: a totals cell's setting is read from its formula, written as LibreOffice saves a structured reference (SUBTOTAL(109,$Sheet.$B$2:$B$5)). As in LibreOffice, a table's name is formed as a defined name's but is apart from them, tables may overlap, and a table may be its header alone.
  • AutoFilters (setAutoFilter, getAutoFilter, removeAutoFilter) take XLSX.jl's arguments and criteria, stored in the sheet's anonymous database range as LibreOffice stores them. Rows are hidden as LibreOffice's Refresh Range hides them, not as Excel does: text LibreOffice reads as a number ("10", "2026-11-23") is compared with numbers by value, list items are compared as text (a date matches only at midnight), and rankings count text and errors. LibreOffice can't store above- or below-average or relative-date filters, so they are errors. removeAutoFilter shows every row of the range, as LibreOffice does.
  • LibreOffice's own options are keywords and fields of the XLSX.jl functions they belong with (D-076): a validation's caseSensitive and sortEntries, cell protection's hideAll and hidePrint, a note's date, a picture's alt and description, any paper size (paperWidth, paperHeight), scaleToPages, six more print options, a table's has_header_row and filterButtons, and a filter's caseSensitive, noDuplicates and regular expressions.
  • Not yet available: conditional formats, charts, and sorts (phase 6).
