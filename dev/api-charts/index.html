API reference: charts

Charts are LibreOffice's embedded chart objects. The chart functions are in a submodule:

using OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts

A few names are also available at the top level: getCharts, getChart, getChartData, getChartRanges, getChartType, getChartSchema and the chart types.

The functions read a chart as it is now: after the edits and structural operations made since the file was read. A series' values are read from its cells, as LibreOffice draws them.

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts — Module
OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts

Charts: LibreOffice's embedded chart objects. Make, format and read a file's charts, with LibreOffice's behaviour.

using OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts

A chart is an object (Object 1/content.xml) framed in a sheet. The functions read it as it is now, after the edits and structural operations made since the file was read.

Creating charts

A chart added here takes LibreOffice's default look (its colours, symbols, fonts and legend). It is a frame anchored in its first cell and resizing with its cells, and its references follow inserts, deletes and sheet operations as LibreOffice moves them.

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts.addChart — Function
addChart(ws::Worksheet, kind::Symbol; anchor, title = nothing, xtitle = nothing, ytitle = nothing,
         subtitle = nothing, threeD = false, deep = false) -> Chart

A new chart of kind over the cells of anchor ("F2:M18"), with no series yet (add them with addSeries): a frame in the first cell, resizing with its cells up to the last, and an object in LibreOffice's default look.

kind is one of :column, :bar, :stackedColumn, :stackedBar, :percentStackedColumn, :percentStackedBar, :line, :lineMarkers, :stackedLine, :percentStackedLine, :stackedLineMarkers, :percentStackedLineMarkers, :area, :stackedArea, :percentStackedArea, :pie, :doughnut, :scatter, :bubble, :radar, :filledRadar, :combo (LibreOffice's column and line chart), :pieOfPie, :barOfPie, and the stock charts :stock (low, high, close), :stockOHLC (open, low, high, close, as candlesticks), :stockVolume and :stockVolumeOHLC (a volume series first, in columns on the left axis). A stock chart's series are added in that order.

title is text (nothing or false: none; LibreOffice has no automatic title). xtitle and ytitle title the axes (not on a pie or doughnut). LibreOffice's titles can't follow a cell: a CellRef is an error.

More options:

  • subtitle; threeD and deep for a 3-D chart; dataTable = true for a data table under the plot;
  • interpolation of line, XY and radar lines: :straight, :smooth, :bspline, :stepStart, :stepEnd, :stepCenterX, :stepCenterY;
  • emptyCells: :gap, :zero or :span (joined over); includeHidden: whether hidden cells are drawn; sortByX: an XY chart's points sorted by x;
  • startAngle of a pie or doughnut: degrees anticlockwise from three o'clock (LibreOffice's; 90 starts at twelve);
  • data, LibreOffice's chart wizard: a range whose series are made at once, in columns or rows (seriesIn = :columns or :rows), the first row and column holding names and categories (firstRowLabels, firstColumnLabels, both true by default).
source
addChart(xf::ODSFile, kind::Symbol; sheetname = "", title = nothing, xtitle = nothing, ytitle = nothing,

A chart on a new chart sheet: LibreOffice's form of one, an ordinary sheet named sheetname (by default Chart1, Chart2, …) holding the chart anchored to the page, after the other sheets. Add its series with addSeries, naming their sheet ("Data!B2:B7"). The other keywords are addChart's, but data (whose cells would be the chart sheet's).

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts.addSeries — Function
addSeries(c::Chart, values; categories = nothing, name = nothing, name_ref = nothing, bubble_sizes = nothing,
          markers = nothing, smooth = nothing, line = nothing, color = nothing, group = nothing) -> Chart

Adds a series to a chart, in LibreOffice's form:

  • values (and categories, name_ref, bubble_sizes) are references: "Data.B2:B7", "Data!B2:B7", "B2:B7" (on the chart's sheet), or a range object.
  • categories are the x axis's (a chart's, not a series': a different range later is an error), or an XY or bubble series' x values. bubble_sizes is required on a bubble chart.
  • name is the series' name as text (loext:label-string), name_ref a cell holding it.
  • markers (true, false or a symbol: :circle, :square, :diamond, :triangle, :x, :plus, :star, :dash, :dot, :none, :auto, and LibreOffice's :arrowDown, :arrowLeft, :bowTie, :hourglass, :loStar, :vBar) on line, radar and XY series; :picture is an error (no picture).
  • smooth on line and XY series: LibreOffice smooths a chart's lines all or none, so a series that differs from those before is an error.
  • line joins an XY series' points.
  • color (hex, a colour name, or a theme colour such as :accent1) is the series' colour (not on a pie or doughnut, whose points are coloured one by one).
  • group (:column or :line) is required on a :combo chart and refused elsewhere.

Histograms and chart sheets

addChartEx makes LibreOffice's histogram; the other "chartex" kinds, which LibreOffice lacks, are errors. A chart sheet is an ordinary sheet holding one chart anchored to the page.

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts.addChartEx — Function
addChartEx(ws::Worksheet, kind::Symbol, values; anchor, categories = nothing, name = nothing,
           name_ref = nothing, title = nothing) -> ChartEx
addChartEx(xf::ODSFile, kind::Symbol, values; sheetname = "", …) -> ChartEx

A chartex chart of kind plotting values, on a sheet or on a new chart sheet. Of the chartex kinds LibreOffice has :histogram, made as LibreOffice makes a histogram; :waterfall, :funnel, :treemap, :sunburst, :pareto and :boxWhisker are errors: LibreOffice reads them without a diagram. A histogram takes no categories, and LibreOffice draws its bins from its values (set them with setSeriesBinning, under setSeriesName).

A chart sheet is LibreOffice's form of one: an ordinary sheet, named sheetname (by default Chart1, Chart2, …), holding the chart anchored to the page. Its references name their sheet ("Data!B2:B7"), since the chart sheet's cells hold nothing.

Changing formatting

The setters change LibreOffice's chart styles, for charts added here and for a file's own charts. Each returns the chart, and :inherit removes a setting. What LibreOffice can't store is an error: compound lines, joins and miter limits; a marker's own fill or border; bubble-size labels; text capitals, kerning sizes and outlines; a minor unit that doesn't divide the major one. Theme colours are resolved against the document's theme: LibreOffice keeps colours in a chart, not links to the theme. A line, XY or radar series has one colour, its line's and its symbols'.

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts.setSeriesFill — Function
setSeriesFill(c::Chart, i, color; transparency = nothing, gradient = nothing, gradientStyle = :linear,

gradientAngle = 0, hatch = nothing, hatchAngle = 0) -> Chart

Series i's fill: a colour (hex, a colour name, a theme colour such as :accent1 or a SchemeColor), :none, or :inherit (LibreOffice's default). Also: transparency, 0 to 100 percent; a gradient from color to gradient (another colour), of gradientStyle (:linear, :axial, :radial, :ellipsoid, :square, :rectangular) at gradientAngle degrees; or a hatch (:single, :double, :triple) of lines of color at hatchAngle degrees. A line, XY or radar series has one colour, its line's and its symbols' (LibreOffice's): its fill is its line colour, and :none, transparency, gradients and hatches are errors.

Sparklines

Sparklines are LibreOffice's sparkline groups. The functions are in the main module (addSparklines, not Charts.addSparklines). A sparkline follows its cell through inserts and deletes, and its data moves as a chart's does.

OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts.addSparklines — Function
addSparklines(ws::Worksheet, location, data; type = :line, kw...)

A group of sparklines, small charts each in one cell: location is the cell, or the row or column of cells, to hold them, and data the cells they chart, on this sheet or another ("Data!B2:M13", "Data.B2:M13"):

  • one cell: one sparkline of a row or column of data;
  • a column of cells: one sparkline for each row of data (as many rows), or each column;
  • a row of cells: one sparkline for each column of data, or each row.

A sparkline already in one of the cells is replaced. The group's options: type (:line, :column, :winloss, which LibreOffice calls stacked), markers, high, low, first, last, negative (true by default for :winloss), axis, emptyCells (:gap, :zero, :span), showHidden, lineWeight (points, 0.75), min and max (:individual, :group or a number), rightToLeft, and the colours seriesColor, negativeColor, axisColor, markersColor, firstColor, lastColor, highColor, lowColor (hex, a colour name, a theme colour or a SchemeColor, resolved against the document's theme).

LibreOffice keeps no date range: dateRange is an error. A sparkline follows its cell through inserts and deletes, and its data moves as a chart's; LibreOffice itself leaves both where they were.
