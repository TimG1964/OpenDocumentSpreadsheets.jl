Creating charts

A first chart

addChart makes a chart over a range of cells, and addSeries adds its series:

using OpenDocumentSpreadsheets
using OpenDocumentSpreadsheets.Charts

xf = newods("Data")
ws = xf["Data"]
ws["A1"] = "Month"; ws["B1"] = "North"; ws["C1"] = "South"
for (i, m) in enumerate(["Jan", "Feb", "Mar", "Apr", "May", "Jun"])
    ws[i+1, 1] = m; ws[i+1, 2] = 10i; ws[i+1, 3] = 40 - 5i
end

c = addChart(ws, :column; anchor = "E2:L18", title = "Sales", xtitle = "Month", ytitle = "£k")
addSeries(c, "B2:B7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "B1")
addSeries(c, "C2:C7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "C1")
writeods("sales.ods", xf)

<!– shot: chartfirst cells=12x19 –> ![Six months of sales for North and South in columns A to C, and beside them a column chart titled Sales with blue columns for North, orange for South, the months along the x axis titled Month, and a y axis titled £k](../images/chartfirst.png)

The frame sits in the anchor's first cell and resizes with its cells up to the last. A series takes its values, and optionally its categories and its name, as references: "B2:B7" on the chart's sheet, or "Data.B2:B7" on another.

The kinds

Kind
:column, :stackedColumn, :percentStackedColumnvertical bars
:bar, :stackedBar, :percentStackedBarhorizontal bars
:line, :lineMarkers, :stackedLine, :percentStackedLine, :stackedLineMarkers, :percentStackedLineMarkerslines, with or without symbols
:area, :stackedArea, :percentStackedAreaareas
:pie, :doughnut, :pieOfPie, :barOfPiepies
:scatter, :bubbleXY charts
:radar, :filledRadarLibreOffice's net charts
:combocolumns and lines
:stock, :stockOHLC, :stockVolume, :stockVolumeOHLCLibreOffice's stock charts
xf = newods("Kinds")
k = xf["Kinds"]
k["A1:C1"] = ["x" "a" "b"]
for i in 1:6
    k[i+1, 1] = i; k[i+1, 2] = i^2 / 4 + 2; k[i+1, 3] = 8 - i
end
for (n, (kind, anchor)) in enumerate([(:column, "E1:J14"), (:stackedBar, "K1:P14"), (:lineMarkers, "Q1:V14"),
                                      (:pie, "E16:J29"), (:scatter, "K16:P29"), (:area, "Q16:V29")])
    ch = addChart(k, kind; anchor, title = string(kind))
    if kind === :scatter
        addSeries(ch, "B2:B7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "B1")
        addSeries(ch, "C2:C7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "C1")
    elseif kind === :pie
        addSeries(ch, "B2:B7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "B1")
    else
        addSeries(ch, "B2:B7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "B1")
        addSeries(ch, "C2:C7"; categories = "A2:A7", name_ref = "C1")
    end
end

Combo charts

makeCombo makes a column or line chart of two or more series LibreOffice's column and line chart: its last series becomes a line on a secondary y axis, the others columns.

xf = newods("Units")
u = xf["Units"]
u["A1:C1"] = ["Quarter" "Units" "Margin"]
u["A2:C5"] = ["Q1" 120 0.21; "Q2" 150 0.24; "Q3" 135 0.19; "Q4" 180 0.27]
c = addChart(u, :column; anchor = "E2:L18", title = "Units and margin")
addSeries(c, "B2:B5"; categories = "A2:A5", name_ref = "B1")
addSeries(c, "C2:C5"; categories = "A2:A5", name_ref = "C1")
makeCombo(c)

LibreOffice's options

addChart takes LibreOffice's chart options too:

  • subtitle;
  • threeD and deep, for a 3-D chart;
  • dataTable, a table of the data under the plot;
  • interpolation: smooth, B-spline and stepped lines;
  • emptyCells (leave a gap, plot zero, or join over) and includeHidden;
  • sortByX, for an XY chart;
  • startAngle, for a pie;
  • data and seriesIn, as LibreOffice's chart wizard: the series of a whole range at once.
c = addChart(ws, :line; anchor = "E20:L36", data = "A1:C7", seriesIn = :columns,
             interpolation = :stepStart, subtitle = "Stepped lines")

LibreOffice's model shows in a few rules:

  • A chart's categories are its x axis's: every series of a non-XY chart shares them.
  • Smoothing is the chart's: all its lines or none.
  • Titles are text.

deleteChart removes a chart and its frame.

Not available

LibreOffice can't store these, so asking for one is an error that says why: titles that follow a cell, and categories of a series' own.
